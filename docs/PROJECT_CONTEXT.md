# Krotitelé nábytku - project context

Updated: 2026-09-05

## What the business does

Krotitelé nábytku buy, restore and sell Czechoslovak furniture, primarily from the 1940s-1970s. The curated sales side focuses on notable Czech/Czechoslovak design and makers such as Halabala, Kropáček & Koželka, Šmídek and Thonet/TON where appropriate.

The business also accepts customer restoration commissions. This service branch may be broader than the curated resale inventory; a piece can be a sensible commission even if it would not be featured as part of the public curated collection.

## Operating model

There are two complementary revenue engines:

1. Curated inventory: buy -> transport -> store -> restore -> photograph -> sell.
2. Customer commissions: customer supplies the piece -> restore -> invoice.

Commission work improves cash flow and does not tie up capital in inventory. Curated stock remains important for brand positioning, references, content and potentially higher-margin sales.

A key operational constraint is restoration capacity. Demand should be managed through pricing and realistic lead times rather than overtime.

## Positioning

The useful differentiation is the combination of:

- trained restoration expertise,
- knowledge of Czechoslovak furniture and attribution,
- purchase + restoration + resale under one brand,
- documentation of labels, manufacturers and model details,
- educational content through the website journal,
- a curated rather than general second-hand assortment.

## Content and SEO

The website has a journal (`/zapisnik/`) used for educational and search-driven content. Existing topics include identification of Šmídek versus the so-called Céčko and the Pavouk side table.

Content should support real expertise rather than generic SEO filler. Manufacturer labels, construction details and attribution evidence can be strong proof of competence when documented accurately.

## Inventory strategy

Internal market tracking has indicated that armchairs are a large and visually attractive category, while cabinets and small storage pieces can turn faster. Tables and standalone dining chairs should be treated more cautiously unless the buy is exceptional or the piece has strong design value.

Use internal tracker / ledger data before suggesting raw purchase prices. Do not infer raw-stock pricing from post-restoration retail asking prices.
