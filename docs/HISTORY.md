# Project history

Updated: 2026-09-05

This is a compact handoff history for future agents. It is not a full accounting ledger and intentionally omits private customer details and sensitive financial information.

## Foundation

Krotitelé nábytku was established around the purchase, restoration and sale of selected Czechoslovak furniture, especially mid-century pieces from roughly the 1940s-1970s. The public website, social profiles and local business listings were built in parallel with the physical restoration workflow.

## Website and discoverability

The website evolved into a clean static site with dedicated pages for furniture, restoration, purchasing furniture and contact. A journal (`/zapisnik/`) was added to publish practical identification and restoration content.

Early journal topics included:

- distinguishing Šmídek armchairs from the so-called Céčko,
- the Pavouk side table and documented manufacturer evidence.

Search visibility started to improve after a period of consistent content and local-profile activity, with new impressions and clicks around restoration-related searches and local queries.

## Attribution and documentation

Manufacturer labels and construction details became an important part of the brand's evidence-based positioning. Examples included documented Interier n. p. Praha VII labeling on a Kropáček & Koželka piece and an UP závody Rousínov label fragment on a Pavouk table.

A standing rule emerged: do not upgrade a probable attribution to a documented one without evidence.

## Market tracking

The business built its own market tracker / scoring workflow for listings. Internal analysis suggested:

- armchairs are a large visible market,
- cabinets and small storage furniture can have faster turnover,
- standalone chairs can clear poorly relative to acquisition cost,
- tables are generally a weak category and should be bought selectively,
- premium curated pieces can justifiably take longer to sell than commodity stock.

This led to a more selective stock strategy and a stronger preference for using the business's own transaction/market data instead of generic asking-price medians.

## Commission work

Paid customer restoration work began before the curated owned inventory had fully validated through completed retail sales. This created a second business engine with better cash-flow characteristics because customer pieces do not require stock acquisition.

The working strategic conclusion became:

- commission work can be broader in style/age than the public curated collection,
- curated stock still matters for brand positioning, references and retail validation,
- workshop capacity is likely to become a more important constraint than demand.

## Operating lessons

Several practical rules were adopted:

- quote lead times conservatively,
- manage excess demand through price rather than overtime,
- document customer pieces on intake,
- use before/after project photography where permission is given,
- inspect partially restored furniture in person before firm pricing when prior work may hide complications,
- preserve some workshop capacity for owned stock so commission work does not stall the original resale model indefinitely.

## Brand voice

The brand voice was deliberately kept natural and conversational. Generic sentimental phrases such as "druhá šance", "s láskou" and "srdcovka" were rejected. Social and email copy should use a short hyphen rather than an em dash and should pass the test of sounding like something a real person would say across a workbench.
