# Current state

Updated: 2026-09-05

This file intentionally contains only operational context that is safe to keep in a public repository. Customer names, private contact details, internal cash-flow figures and sensitive pending-deal data are excluded.

## Business stage

The business is now receiving paid restoration commissions in addition to selling/restoring owned stock. Early demand suggests that workshop capacity may become the main bottleneck before customer demand does.

## Current priorities

1. Finish and stabilize workshop capacity.
2. Keep customer commissions moving without freezing renovation of owned inventory.
3. Validate the curated resale model with the first completed-stock sale(s).
4. Continue journal/SEO content based on real restoration expertise.
5. Improve local-search conversion and service information on the web/profile.
6. Keep measuring inquiry source and realized job economics.

## Operational guardrails

- Quote realistic lead times with margin.
- As demand rises, use price to manage capacity rather than overtime.
- For partially restored pieces, inspect in person before committing to a firm price where hidden prior work may affect scope.
- For each commission, record before photos, agreed scope, visible defects, price basis, timeline and any later change in scope.
- Ask permission before using customer-project photos publicly.
- Keep at least some capacity flowing into owned inventory so commission work does not indefinitely block the resale side.

## Website/content state

The journal is live and should continue as an expertise channel. Priority future content includes practical restoration topics where the workshop can provide a real expert position, rather than generic content written only for keywords.

## Data discipline

The business already maintains operational/feedback data outside this repository (including a Google Sheet). Before proposing changes to pricing, lead handling or market scoring, use the freshest available data rather than relying on this snapshot.
